Timing complications

Four ways a chronograph earns its keep

Chronographs were born as working instruments for pilots, racing teams, doctors and sports officials. Each variation solves a specific timing problem.

Aviation

Flyback

One press stops, resets and restarts the chronograph instantly, so a pilot can time successive legs without three separate pushes.

Motorsport

Tachymeter

A bezel scale that converts elapsed time over a known distance, such as one kilometre or mile, into average speed.

Weather & distance

Telemeter

Measures distance using the speed of sound: start at the flash, stop at the thunder, and read the distance on the scale.

Athletics

Split-seconds

Also called rattrapante: a second chronograph hand can be stopped to record an intermediate time while the other keeps running.

The art of blue

How watch dials turn blue

Blue is one of the most loved dial colours, and there are several very different ways to make it. Each technique changes how the dial behaves in the light.

Sunburst

Fine radial lines brushed outward from the centre catch the light and make the blue shift from bright to almost black as the wrist moves.

Inside the chronograph

What happens when you press start

  1. Control system. A column wheel or cam decides whether the chronograph is running, stopped or resetting.

  2. Clutch. A horizontal or vertical clutch connects the chronograph to the running movement.

  3. Counters. The central hand counts seconds; subdials count minutes and sometimes hours.

  4. Reset hammers. Hammers strike heart-shaped cams to snap each hand back to zero.

  5. Flyback lever. In a flyback, pressing reset while running briefly disengages, resets and re-engages in one motion.

A short history

From racecourse timers to the wrist

The chronograph began as a scientific and sporting instrument in the nineteenth century and moved to the wrist in the early twentieth, where it became indispensable to aviators and racing drivers.

  1. 1820sEarly chronographs time horse races and astronomical observations.
  2. 1910sWrist chronographs appear, with a single pusher in the crown.
  3. 1930sTwo-pusher layouts and the flyback function arrive, favoured by pilots.
  4. 1969Automatic chronograph movements are introduced by several makers.

Chronograph questions

Still curious about a pusher, scale or dial? Ask us and we’ll explain.

Ask a question
What is a flyback chronograph?

A flyback chronograph lets you reset and restart timing with a single press of the reset pusher while the chronograph is running. On a standard chronograph you must stop, reset and start again, which takes three presses.

How do I use a tachymeter?

Start the chronograph as you pass a marker, such as a kilometre post, and stop it at the next one. The number on the tachymeter scale opposite the seconds hand is your average speed in units per hour. It works for elapsed times up to about 60 seconds.

Can I start and stop my chronograph whenever I like?

Yes, chronographs are designed for regular use. Just avoid pressing the pushers underwater unless the watch is specifically rated for it, as that can let water in.

Does running the chronograph wear the movement?

Running the chronograph uses some energy and causes some wear, like any mechanism. Modern column-wheel and vertical-clutch designs are built for frequent use, and regular servicing keeps them healthy.

Why are blue dials so popular?

Blue is versatile: it reads almost black in low light and vivid in sunshine, and it pairs with steel, gold, leather and bracelets. Techniques like sunburst finishing make blue dials especially lively.
