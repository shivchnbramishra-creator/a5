The complete chronograph guide

How chronographs work, how to read and operate one, the main types and scales, and how to look after a watch that measures time as well as telling it.

Reading the dial

Anatomy of a chronograph

  • Central chronograph hand: usually thin and brightly coloured, it sweeps once a minute while timing and rests at 12 otherwise.
  • Running seconds: often a small subdial, showing the watch is working even when the chronograph is stopped.
  • Minute counter: typically records up to 30 or 60 minutes of elapsed time.
  • Hour counter: on some watches, records up to 12 hours.
  • Pushers: at 2 o’clock to start and stop, at 4 o’clock to reset.

Mechanisms

Column wheel vs cam, horizontal vs vertical clutch

Column wheels are castellated wheels that rotate step by step to coordinate the start, stop and reset levers. They give a crisp, consistent pusher feel and are prized for their craftsmanship. Cam-actuated chronographs use a shaped cam instead; they are robust, efficient to produce and very reliable.

A horizontal clutch connects the chronograph via meshing gears, which can make the hand jump slightly on start. A vertical clutch uses friction discs stacked on one axis, giving a smooth start and allowing the chronograph to run continuously with less wear.

Start, stop, reset

Always stop the chronograph before resetting, unless your watch is a flyback designed for resetting on the fly.

Water and pushers

Don’t operate pushers underwater unless the watch is rated and designed for it; screw-down pushers must be screwed in first.

Running it all day

Vertical-clutch chronographs are designed to run continuously, while some older designs prefer occasional use.

In the cockpit

Why pilots loved the flyback

Navigating by dead reckoning meant timing each leg of a flight precisely. When an aircraft turned onto a new heading, the navigator needed to start timing again immediately.

With a flyback, a single press of the reset pusher returns the hand to zero and restarts it, saving seconds and reducing the chance of error at a busy moment.

Scales

Tachymeter, telemeter and pulsometer

A tachymeter converts the seconds taken to cover a known distance into speed. A telemeter uses the speed of sound to estimate distance, traditionally to lightning or artillery. A pulsometer was designed for doctors to count pulse beats over a set number of beats.

Each scale is calibrated for a particular calculation. Try them in our interactive calculators.

Care

Looking after a chronograph

  • Have mechanical chronographs serviced at the interval recommended by the maker.
  • Keep pushers and crown free of dirt and sand; rinse with fresh water if the watch is water resistant.
  • Avoid strong magnetic fields from speakers, laptops and bag clasps.
  • If the chronograph hand doesn’t return exactly to zero, ask a watchmaker to adjust it.
